Install in ten seconds

One bash file. No dependencies — everything it uses ships with macOS. Uninstall = delete the file.

$ curl -fsSL https://raw.githubusercontent.com/bnishit/machogs/main/machogs -o /usr/local/bin/machogs && chmod +x /usr/local/bin/machogs
$ brew install bnishit/tap/machogs

Then just run machogs. It only looks — it never closes anything on its own.

The questions you actually typed to get here

Why is my Mac fan so loud?

Almost always: one program is stuck using a whole CPU core. The fan is not broken — it is doing its job, cooling a chip that something is cooking. The hard part is that the program is usually one you never opened and cannot name. In Activity Monitor it shows up as bun, or node, or Helper (Renderer). Those names tell you nothing, so most people scroll past and reboot instead. machogs names the app that left it there.

Why is my MacBook so hot?

Same cause, different symptom. A chip running flat out gets hot, and the metal case passes that heat to your hands. If your Mac is hot while you are only writing an email, something is running that you did not ask for.

Why is my battery draining so fast?

A stuck program does not sleep. It keeps a CPU core awake, which stops your Mac from entering its low-power states. The battery estimate drops from nine hours to three, and nothing on screen explains it. Same problem as the loud fan, arriving through a different door.

Why is my Mac suddenly slow?

Two different causes, and it is worth knowing which one you have. Either something is eating the CPU — machogs finds this and can fix it — or you have run out of fast memory and your Mac is shuffling memory to disk, in which case closing programs barely helps and machogs will tell you plainly: restart, because nothing else fixes it.

Why is my Mac storage full?

Usually dead weight in predictable places: app caches, Docker's disk image, old iPhone backups, Xcode build junk, npm caches, the Trash, Downloads. machogs disk is the storage X-ray — it measures the usual junk spots and tells you which are safe to clear and how. It points; you decide. And when you decide, machogs disk clear ~/.npm (or two taps on Clear in the app) empties a safe cache and reports exactly how much came back — refusing any path that is not on its own safe list.

"Port 3000 is already in use" — by what??

The error every dev knows, and it never says who. machogs port 3000 tells you exactly who is squatting it, in which project folder, for how long — and machogs port 3000 kill frees it. It also knows the famous one: macOS's own AirPlay Receiver sits on ports 5000 and 7000, killing it doesn't stick, and machogs points you at the setting that actually turns it off. Same honesty for background services — a Homebrew database, a device agent — where a kill would just respawn in a second: machogs says so and hands you the command that actually stops it.

Can my Mac warn me when something starts eating the CPU?

Now, yes. Machogs.app watches every minute, and the moment something has been cooking a core for two checks in a row — or an AI coding session ends and leaves helpers running, or one app clones the same helper past reason — a small black pill slides out from under the notch with a one-line verdict and a Close it button. Once per problem, with a cooldown, and a bell toggle if you'd rather it never speaks.

Why your Mac is lowkey always busy

Your Mac used to run the programs you opened. That is no longer true. Today it runs the programs you opened, the helpers those programs started, the AI assistants you gave access to, and the background servers those assistants start so they can search the web and read your files. You did not open any of them. You cannot name them. And they leak.

Every "clean your Mac" app ever written deletes caches to free disk space. That solves a problem from 2010. machogs deletes nothing — it looks at what is running, and it knows the names of the AI tools that leave things behind.

It will not eat your homework

  • Never closes anything on its own. Running machogs only looks. machogs fix asks you about every single item.
  • Never closes a coding session. Claude Code sessions and their helpers are listed and skipped — you do not lose unsaved work.
  • Only touches your own programs. macOS system processes are out of scope entirely.
  • Leaves real work alone — video encoders, backups, Spotlight indexing are exempt.
  • Everything is logged to ~/Library/Logs/machogs.log, and machogs blame turns the log into a scoreboard of which app leaks most on your machine. 👑

Suspicious? Good. Run machogs --check — it audits the safety rules and closes nothing.
